Sunday is a quiet one in the listings, which makes it a good day to plan the week, because the week is busy.

The Aberdeen Comedy Festival takes over the city's stages from Tuesday. Maisie Adam opens at the Tivoli and is already sold out, and from there the Lemon Tree carries most of the programme: Alasdair Beckett-King on Wednesday, Andrew Ryan and Mark Thomas on Thursday, and a packed Friday with Kim Blythe, Craig Hill and a late-night show. Susan Calman's Music Hall date on Friday has sold out too. The full list, with venues, is on the What's On page.

If you would rather sing along than laugh, the Tivoli runs tribute nights from Wednesday to Saturday, from Pink Floyd to ABBA, and Scottish Ballet brings Emergence to His Majesty's on Friday and Saturday. Next weekend, the Getaway Show fills the P&J Arena for three days, and it is on the Culture page too.

Between shows, there is a list worth keeping. Nine food and drink places opened in Aberdeen this summer, from a Brazilian restaurant on Union Street to three trailers on the Esplanade. The Food & Drink page has all nine.

On campus, the University of Aberdeen and NHS Grampian have opened what the university calls the UK's first General Practice and Mental Health Teaching Centre, and on the pitch the Dons took a point at Easter Road to keep their unbeaten run going.

The weather desk has the week turning wetter from Monday, so the first half of the week favours an indoor plan, and the forecast is inside.
