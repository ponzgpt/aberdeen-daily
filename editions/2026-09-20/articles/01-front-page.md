The biggest story for the region this weekend came from Holyrood rather than from the city itself. Michael Marra, one of the MSPs elected for the North East, was named on Saturday as the new leader of Scottish Labour, taking over from Anas Sarwar. It is not every year that a party leader answers to voters in this corner of Scotland, and the paper will report what he says about the issues that land here once he says it. His profile and the announcement are on the Council & Politics page.

On the pitch, Aberdeen drew 1–1 at Hibernian. Kevin Nisbet's twelfth goal of the season kept the unbeaten run going, and with a cup semi-final already secured it was, in Stephen Robinson's words, a good week, if one with more work to come. The details are on the Sport page.

Not all of the week's news was comfortable. Talks over a £10m savings plan at a university, one that could cost more than a hundred jobs, broke down after a union official was dismissed. Administrators dealing with Brewdog after its takeover say there is not enough money to pay everyone owed, staff wages among them. Both stories are thin tonight, and both are reported as far as the record goes and no further.

Offshore, survey work is starting along the cable corridor of the Bellrock floating wind farm, a small but concrete step in the energy transition the city watches closely. The markets board has Brent crude and the three London energy names this paper follows.

Plan the week around Monday. The weather desk has it as both the warmest and the wettest day in Aberdeen, so it is a day for a coat rather than a long walk; the forecast and its board are inside.
